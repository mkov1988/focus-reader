SHEET 02

What's down there now

FROM PHOTOS · AUG 2026

Good bones, honestly: dry-looking gray block, open joists, a black rack already doing the aesthetic's heavy lifting, laundry machines on the plumbing wall, and a half-built partition that just needs mud, paint, and a door. The middle zone is storage — fridges, coolers, and pantry racks all leave for the garage before anything else happens.

Keeping

  • Power rack + rings, bar, plates, bench, heavy bag — the gym seed
  • Folding treadmill — parks against the long wall
  • Washer + dryer and the laundry plumbing wall as-is
  • The new partition — gets finished, insulated, painted, doored
  • Block walls + open ceiling — the look, not a problem to hide

Unknowns that shape the plan

  • True dimensions — 30–40' long, 10–15' wide; everything here scales
  • Headroom — joist height, and the duct that dips at the laundry end
  • Moisture — 48-hour plastic test decides paint type and flooring prep
  • Panel capacity + furnace/water-heater location — not in the photos
  • Windows? — none visible; one would flip the layout to B

Rule for every tier: nothing gets painted, glued, or framed until the moisture test passes and the tape measure has been down there. That trip is Sheet 08.

SHEET 07

Keep the block, or finish it?

THE STANDING QUESTION

It's cinder block (CMU), not brick — and painted dark, it looks right for this. The answer at every level: paint the block in the gym, and only spend on framing where a room needs to feel soft and warm — the office at Tier 2, everything at Tier 3.

OptionCost (~770 sq ft walls)You getWatch out
Paint it$400–800 DIYHonest industrial; coursing shows; reversibleMoisture test first — damp block wants breathable mineral paint or limewash, never a sealing film
Skim / parge, then limewash$1–2/sq ft DIY · $3–6 proSoft, monolithic, "old European gym" look — good middle optionLabor-heavy; still wants breathable finish
Fur + insulate + drywall$7–12/sq ft installedFully finished, warm, hides wiringLoses 3–5" per side of a narrow room; moisture detailing must be right

Before any of them: the 48-hour plastic-sheet moisture test, and a walk around the outside (gutters, downspouts, grade). $0–300 of exterior fixes beats $3K of interior regret. If the test comes back wet, that's a stop-and-reassess — not a paint-over.

SHEET 08

Next steps

PHASE 0 · ~2 HRS + 48H WAITS · ~$50

The measuring trip

  1. Tape measure: true length + width, slab-to-joist, slab-to-lowest-duct (both ends)
  2. Find + photograph: panel (cover open), furnace, water heater, behind the white door, any windows, any floor drain
  3. Moisture test: plastic squares taped to wall ×2 + slab ×1, read at 48h
  4. Radon kit in the gym end (people will live down here now)
  5. Scratch-test the tan floor in a corner — paint or overlay?

Decisions on the table

  1. Tier — 1, 2, 3, or "1 now, wired for 2"
  2. Walls — paint / skim+limewash / frame (rec: paint the gym, frame the office at T2+)
  3. Layout — A, unless a window turns up (then B)
  4. Divider — curtain now vs glass line — the single biggest look-per-dollar call

Measurements come back → the plan and these mockups get re-scaled to reality, and the chosen tier gets a real shopping list.
